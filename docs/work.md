# The work: form and material

This document describes the music of *Night Music for Orchestra*.
It gives the form, the themes, and the sources of the ideas.
The style study ([`style-study.md`](style-study.md)) gives the measured values that the form uses.

## Summary

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Form | One movement in five linked parts |
| Length | 595 bars, 34.1 minutes |
| Tonic | D |
| Orchestra | 99 players (see [`design/orchestra.toml`](../design/orchestra.toml)) |
| Score | In C. Instruments that sound one or two octaves from the written note keep that difference. |

The music has no programme and no titles for the parts.
The form is a journey from dark to light: D minor with a tritone at the start, D major at the end.

## The motto

One idea of three notes is in all parts of the work.
It has four states.
Each state changes one interval of the state before it.

| State | Notes on D | Intervals (semitones) | Where |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dark | D, E-flat, A-flat | 1, then 5 (a tritone from D) | Parts 1 and 2 |
| Twilight | D, E-flat, A | 1, then 6 (a fifth from D) | Part 3 (on A-flat: A-flat, A, E-flat) |
| Shimmer | D, E, G-sharp | 2, then 4 (a tritone from D) | Part 4, from the turn |
| Light | D, E, A | 2, then 5 (a fifth from D) | Part 4 (after the climax) and part 5 |

The note A-flat is the pivot of the work.

- In the dark state, A-flat goes down to G.
- In the shimmer state, the same pitch has the name G-sharp. It goes up to A.
- In part 5, the pitch between G and G-sharp is partial 11 of the harmonic series on D. It is a part of the sound of the tonic.

## The themes

| Theme | First time | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walking theme | Bar 1, violas | Five notes up from D to A-flat. The cellos have its mirror form. |
| Long song | Mark A, English horn | A free melody around A and G-sharp, in the manner of a Carpathian long song. |
| Chorale | Mark B, four muted horns | Four chords. The highest voice is the motto and its fall to G. |
| Agitated theme | Mark D, violas and cellos | The motto as leaps, after a rest on the strong beat. |
| Song theme | Mark F, clarinet | The long song in a regular metre. In F minor, then in B minor. |
| Chase theme | Mark G (bar 5), clarinet and violas | The walking theme, fast, in 7/8. Each bar is a minor third higher. |
| Heavy riff | Mark M, low instruments | The notes of the motto as a riff of two bars in 7/8. The drums, the horns, and the high strings play in its gaps. |
| Warm theme | Mark AA, cellos | A broad melody in D major. It starts with the motto in its shimmer state. |
| Hammer theme of the piano | Mark W (bar 7), piano | The cell A-flat, A, D, E-flat (two tritones a semitone apart) in alternate hands, with accents in the rhythm 3 + 3 + 2. |
| Theme in the forge | Mark X, piano | The notes of the warm theme with the dark form of the motto (D, E-flat, A-flat, G; B-flat, A-flat, G-flat, E-flat), in hammered octaves. |

After the turn, the themes of the dark parts come back in major keys:

- The song theme is in F-sharp major (mark BB, bar 5) and in D major (mark FF, bar 9, one horn).
- The agitated theme is a warm line of the cellos (mark CC, bar 9).
- The walking theme goes up through the major scale (mark DD).
- The chorale is in D major (mark FF, bar 17, four horns; mark KK, the full orchestra).
- The heavy riff is in D major, with the light state of the motto (mark JJ).

In part 5 (mark HH), the warm theme and the song theme sound at the same time.
The walking theme and the chase theme go with them.

A bar number in this document is a bar of the conductor score.
A place such as "mark BB, bar 5" is the fifth bar from the rehearsal mark BB.

## Targets and the work

The form plan has targets from the style study.
The command `uv run nightmusic stats` measures the work.
The loudness of a moment is the loudest dynamic mark in effect at that moment.

| Value | Target | The work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Share of the time: p or softer | 0.55 to 0.68 | 0.46 |
| Share of the time: mp or mf | 0.08 to 0.14 | 0.16 |
| Share of the time: f or louder | 0.18 to 0.28 | 0.37 |
| Bars for each tempo mark | 5 to 13 | 9.9 |

The share of the soft levels is a little below the target, and the share of the loud levels is above the target.
The causes are the heavy sections of parts 2 and 5, and the ending with the full orchestra, fff.
The owner of the project selected them.

## The keys

- Before the turn, the centres move by minor thirds: D, F, A-flat, B.
  All these centres are in one scale of alternate semitones and tones. This scale contains the dark state of the motto.
- Part 3 is on A-flat, a tritone from the tonic. It ends on E-flat, one semitone above the tonic.
- After the turn, the centres move by major thirds: D, F-sharp, B-flat.
- D-flat, one semitone below the tonic, comes before the main climax.
  Thus the tonic has a neighbour on each side: E-flat before the turn and D-flat before the main climax.

## Ideas from other traditions

The work uses ideas from four traditions. It does not quote melodies.

| Tradition | Idea | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carpathian and Balkan | The long song: a free melody with ornaments. The cimbalom. | Part 1 (mark A) |
| Carpathian and Balkan | The uneven metre 2 + 2 + 3. | Part 2 (marks G to M) and part 5 (mark JJ) |
| Arab | A melody in the manner of a night call. Its second note is a neutral second (a quarter-tone). | Part 3 (mark T) |
| Javanese and Balinese | A cycle of gongs: a large gong starts each cycle, smaller gongs divide it. | Part 3 (marks S to V) |
| Javanese and Balinese | Two instruments play alternate notes of one fast line. | Parts 4 and 5 (celesta and harp) |
| Japanese court music | The high chord of the mouth organ, with slow breaths. | Part 3 (accordion) |
| Hindustani | A drone on the tonic and the fourth. A slow melody with a slow change of pitch on one note. | Part 3 (mark V) |

## The percussion

The orchestra has one timpani player and six percussion players.

| Place | Instruments |
| --- | --- |
| The heavy sections (marks M and FF) | Bass drum, taiko, tom-toms, snare drum, cymbals, timpani. In part 5 also tubular bells. |
| The way up to the first climax (mark P) | Tom-toms and taiko: their strokes come nearer and nearer together. Rolls of the bass drum and of the suspended cymbal. |
| The first climax (mark Q) | Hand cymbals, suspended cymbal, tam-tam, bass drum, taiko, tubular bells, timpani. |
| The main climax (mark EE) | Hand cymbals, suspended cymbal, tam-tam, triangle, bass drum, tubular bells, glockenspiel, timpani. |
| The last statement (mark KK) | Hand cymbals, suspended cymbal, tam-tam, bass drum, taiko, tubular bells, glockenspiel, timpani. |

A player who goes to a different instrument has 4 seconds or more for the change.
The timpani player has 5 seconds or more to tune a drum to a different note (the check uses four drums).
The command `nightmusic check` makes sure of these two items.

## How the code makes the music

The music is Python code in the directory [`src/nightmusic/work`](../src/nightmusic/work).
Each part is one file.
A theme is written one time, as text in a subset of the LilyPond language.
Functions make the other forms of a theme:

| Function | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `tr`, `octave` | Move the music to a different pitch. |
| `invert` | Make the mirror form. |
| `lines` | Divide a chord progression into its voices. |
| `fill` | Make a figure from each chord of a progression. |
| `scatter` | Put short figures at given moments, with rests between them. |
| `Block` | Collect the music of a section phrase by phrase. |

The command `nightmusic check` finds notes that are out of the range of an instrument.
It also finds a change of instrument, and a change of tuning of the timpani, for which the player has too little time.

The command `nightmusic docs` writes the bar numbers, the times, and the measured values of the work into this document and into the file `README.md`.
A test makes sure that these values are up to date.

## Limits

- The composer of the code cannot hear the audio. The checks of the audio are measurements: loudness, tone balance, and stereo width.
- The parts do not have cue notes.
- The audio does not play trills as trills.
- Some instruments use the samples of a similar instrument. The file [`design/brief.toml`](../design/brief.toml) lists them.
